Books & Resources

Curated material for NMTC preparation — from official sources to books and free online practice.

Official

  • AMTI — amtionline.com — the official home of the NMTC: notifications, registration details, dates and results.
  • AMTI publications — the AMTI itself publishes books of previous years' NMTC question papers with solutions for each level. These are the single best practice source; order them via the AMTI website or ask your school to.
  • Your school's mathematics teacher — schools register as NMTC centres, so your teacher is the fastest route to enrolment and often has past papers on hand.

Recommended books

Book Best for Why
NMTC previous-year papers (AMTI) All levels Nothing beats the real question style; solutions included.
Mathematical Circles (Fomin, Genkin, Itenberg) Sub-Junior / Junior The classic gentle introduction to olympiad thinking, organised topic-wise.
Challenge and Thrill of Pre-College Mathematics (Krishnamurthy et al.) Junior / Inter Written by Indian olympiad mentors; covers exactly the algebra–geometry–number theory core NMTC draws on.
Pathfinder for Olympiad Mathematics (Krishna & Pandey) Junior / Inter Large graded problem sets for PRMO/RMO-style contests.
Elementary Number Theory (David Burton) Inter Solid grounding in congruences and classical number theory.
Problem-Solving Strategies (Arthur Engel) Inter (advanced) The deep end — invariants, pigeonhole, extremal principle. Use selectively.

Free online material

🌐 Art of Problem Solving

artofproblemsolving.com — huge free problem archives (AMC/AIME style overlaps well with NMTC), a wiki of theorems, and active forums where NMTC problems are discussed and solved.

🇮🇳 Indian olympiad ecosystem

HBCSE olympiads portal — past IOQM/RMO papers are excellent extra practice for Junior and Inter NMTC levels, and show where the pathway leads.

📺 Khan Academy

khanacademy.org/math — free, class-wise lessons to patch any gap the Syllabus page exposes before you move to contest-level problems.

🧩 Project-style practice

NRICH (University of Cambridge) — beautifully curated puzzle-style problems by age group; ideal for Primary and Sub-Junior levels.
